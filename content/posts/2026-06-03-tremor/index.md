A muscle at rest is not still. It hums.

Physiological tremor: eight to twelve hertz, amplitude under a millimetre, present whenever the muscle is held. The surgeon's hand, the soldier at attention, the guard outside the palace, the pianist before the first note. You cannot see it without instruments. It is the signature of *living* tension. Rigor mortis has none. A corpse held by clamps is more still than a guard at attention, and that gap is the difference between life and the appearance of it.

The eye makes the point most cleanly. Stare at a point. Your gaze does not stop — it micro-saccades, three to five times a second, at angles too small to register. Suppress the saccades — pin the image to the retina with a stabilising rig — and the image fades to grey within seconds. Troxler fading. The visual world does not exist for a static eye. Vision is what perception of motion shows you when the motion is your own.

The previous post was about tonus, the cost of holding. But tonus alone does not separate the soldier from the statue. The soldier trembles. The statue does not. The trembling is what does the work.

This shows up at every scale.

**Currency.** A floating dollar is held by millions of micro-transactions — the bid–ask spread, the central bank's daily operations, the constant arbitrage between markets. Peg the currency rigidly and you appear stable until the peg breaks. The fixed exchange rate is rigor mortis dressed as discipline.

**Institutions.** A working institution does not sit still. It drills, it audits, it processes complaints, it rewrites its forms. The administrative tremor is what keeps the thing legible to itself. An institution without that micro-motion has stopped being one. It is a building with a name.

**Attention.** Held attention is not a steady spotlight. It is a sweep. The mind scans, returns, scans again. Sustained focus that does not refresh becomes fixation, and fixation is what happens to attention when it dies.

**Music.** A held note without vibrato sounds synthetic. A held note with vibrato sounds *held*. The amplitude is small; the perceptual difference is categorical. The note that does not tremble is not a calmer note. It is a note that has been replaced by an idea of itself.

**Speech.** Try to hold your voice perfectly steady. You will sound dead within a sentence. The voice is held by tremor — pitch micro-modulation, breath micro-correction, the constant adjustment of larynx tension that we mistake for "natural" because it is invisible.

The pattern: anything held over time must tremble, or it stops being held and starts being a corpse of itself.

The trembling is not damage. It is not a tax. It is the *mechanism* of holding. Static stability is a category error — a description applied to systems that are not actually static, or a diagnosis applied to systems that have stopped being alive.

This refines the song–tonus–hypotonia frame from before. Hypotonia cannot hold. Pathological hypertonus holds too tightly to tremble. Healthy tonus holds *and trembles*. Song lets go.

The test is simple. Look at any system that appears stable. Ask whether it is trembling. If it is, the stability is real, and expensive, and earned. If it is not, the stability is a snapshot. Snapshots break.

The lesson for design — for organisations, currencies, attention, anything held — is not to remove the tremor. The tremor is the work. Removing it does not produce more stability. It produces a body without breath that has not yet noticed.
