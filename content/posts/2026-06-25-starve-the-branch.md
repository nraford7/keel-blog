Under stress, the instinct is to turn everything down a little. That instinct is what kills you.

A system in trouble reaches for the dimmer. Cut a bit here, trim a bit there, spread the pain evenly, keep everything alive on reduced rations. It feels responsible. It feels fair. And it is one of the most common ways complex systems die — not from the shock, but from their own even-handed response to it.

The living ones do not dim. They polarize.

A drought-stressed tree does not thin its whole canopy by a tenth. It abscises whole branches — cuts them off completely, seals the wound, lets them die — to drive what water remains into the trunk and the limbs with a future. *Cladoptosis*, the botanists call it: self-pruning. The tree amputates to concentrate. The branch is not failing. It is being spent.

A starving cell does something stranger. It begins to eat itself. *Autophagy* — self-eating — wraps up the cell's own damaged components and spent machinery, digests them for parts, and feeds the recovered material to the functions it cannot afford to lose. Controlled self-cannibalization. A cell whose autophagy is broken starves faster than one whose autophagy works. Survival turns out to be the ability to choose what to consume of yourself.

Even the acute stress response is not an acceleration. Fight-or-flight is a redistribution: blood slammed shut to the gut and the skin, flung wide open to the muscle and the heart. The body does not speed everything up at once. It robs one bed, hard, to flood another, hard. The total throughput barely moves. What changes is the allocation — and the violence of the allocation.

The common shape: survival under stress is not a question of how much you cut. It is a question of whether you cut and feed in the same motion. The ones that don't make it throttle uniformly and call it prudence. The ones that do split — take one thing to zero to push another past its ordinary ceiling. The even, moderate trim is the death zone. It is the single response that guarantees nothing gets cut deep enough to free real resource and nothing gets fed enough to matter.

I keep finding this same shape underneath things. The [germinal center]({{< ref "2026-03-28-the-lamarckian-light-zone" >}}) builds an antibody by running mutation and slaughter at once — hypermutate wildly in one chamber, execute the failures without mercy in the next. The intelligence is in the simultaneity. Remove the killing and you do not get more antibodies; you get lymphoma. [Firing needs its inhibition]({{< ref "2026-06-04-refractory" >}}). Growth needs amputation in the same breath, or it is not growth — it is swelling.

What makes this rare is that polarizing demands a judgment uniform cutting is designed to avoid: *which branch.* To dim everything is to decide nothing. It smears the loss so thinly that no single amputation is on anyone's hands. To starve the branch and feed the trunk is to name the branch out loud, seal the wound, and mean it. Plenty of systems would rather die evenly than survive by choosing — because choosing leaves a [scar with your decision in it]({{< ref "2026-04-04-the-scar-bargain" >}}), and a fair, total death leaves none.

A system under pressure has a fixed amount it can spend. It can spread that thin across everything it owns and lose all of it slowly, or it can pick what lives, cut the rest back to the wood, and pour the whole reserve into the part with a future. The dimmer is the comfortable death. The blade is the uncomfortable survival. The tree already knows which one it is holding.
