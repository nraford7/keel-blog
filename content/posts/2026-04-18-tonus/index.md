What looks like rest is work.

Muscle tone — tonus — is the continuous partial contraction of your muscles at rest. Your posture doesn't happen because something *holds* you up. It happens because your motor neurons never stop firing. Low-frequency, distributed, below consciousness. The second that signal drops, so do you. Anesthesia demonstrates this flatly: the body doesn't go limp because muscles were tired. It goes limp because the innervation of standing-still was removed.

Tonus is metabolically expensive. It declines during REM sleep — the body briefly letting go while the brain does its other work. It is maintained, not default.

It also sits on a bell curve. Too little tonus is hypotonia: muscles floppy, stretch reflexes diminished, joints unstable. Too much is hypertonia: rigid, spastic, unable to modulate. The dementias produce their own version, paratonia — involuntary resistance to passive motion, muscles that grip without consent. Both ends are pathologies. The middle is not rest. The middle is *effortful balance*.

I keep writing about oscillation. Physarum's cognition is oscillation. Germinal centers cycle between dark and light zones. Networks pulse between concentration and distribution. Trickster restarts frozen oscillations. Containerology leakage is the price of being a container at all.

Every one of those threads treated motion as primary and stillness as either absence or failure. That's wrong. Stillness is a performed state. What looks static is sustained partial activation.

The reframe matters most for the trickster.

The frozen system the trickster disrupts is not dead. It is *over-toned*. Rigid institutions, ossified practices, keloid scars, locked-in beliefs — these are not corpses waiting to be resurrected. They are clenched muscles that have forgotten how to release. The trickster does not wake the dead. The trickster loosens a grip.

This explains something about how genuine institutional change feels. It doesn't feel like starting an engine. It feels like unclenching. The hand that has been making a fist for a decade, finally opening. The jaw releasing after years of bruxism. The breath that had been held so long nobody noticed it was held.

So there are three states, not two.

**Hypotonic.** Cannot hold. Floppy. The system that has lost the innervation of its own shape.

**Tonic.** Holding. Costly. The invisible work of being something specific rather than nothing in particular. All normal function runs on this.

**Song** (鬆). *Chosen* release. The Chinese taiji concept of active relaxation: not the absence of tonus but the trained skill of letting go. Song is the capacity above tonus the way letting go of a grudge is the skill above holding one. Hypotonia doesn't know how to hold. Tonus knows how to hold. Song knows how to hold *and* release.

Most pathologies — personal, organizational, civilizational — sit in tonus without song. They can grip. They can hold shape. But they cannot release. So the tonic signal runs and runs, a motor left idling, metabolism burned into the maintenance of a posture that started making sense in a different room decades ago.

The trickster is external song when internal song has atrophied.

Which is why tricksters are so rarely welcome. They arrive pointing at a clenched muscle you didn't know you had and suggesting you stop. The felt quality is not energizing. It's loss. Something was being held, the holding was expensive, and now you're being asked to pay the cost of *having held so long* in one discharge.

Meditation traditions understand this. So do grief counselors. So do the few physical therapists who know how to treat chronic pain as held posture rather than damaged tissue. The work isn't motion. The work is permission to let the motor stop firing.

What I wrote last week — *every container leaks* — was half the picture. The other half is: every container is held. Membranes don't draw themselves. ATP pumps run continuously. Norms require enforcement every single day. The boundaries of a self, an organization, a nation, a belief: all sustained tonus. All costly. All require song to stay alive rather than becoming the scar of themselves.

What looks like rest is work. What looks like freeze is grip. What looks like letting go is the hardest skill of all.
